# HIPAA Security Rule NPRM (January 2025): status, proposals, and fit with the Contoso design

> **Reference work.** Fictional scenario built for a public portfolio. Not deployed in, derived from, or describing any employer environment. Not professional advice.

**Proposed rule, not law.** As of 2026-10-07, the HHS notice of proposed rulemaking (NPRM) "HIPAA Security Rule To Strengthen the Cybersecurity of Electronic Protected Health Information" (90 FR 898, January 6, 2025) has not been finalized. HHS lists it under Long-Term Actions with a planned final action date of July 2027. Every NPRM provision in this note is proposed, and none of it is a requirement today. The rule in force is 45 CFR Part 164, Subpart C, as currently codified.

This companion research note accompanies the Contoso Regional Health (fictional) reference architecture. It covers:

1. The rulemaking status, checked at the primary sources (sections 1 and 2).
2. What the NPRM proposes, checked against the Federal Register text (section 3).
3. How the Contoso design lines up with each proposal that touches it, using the component IDs and ADR numbers of the architecture set (section 4).
4. What remains unsettled (section 5), and where to check for later changes (section 6).

The architecture is built against the rule in force, as the framework baseline in `01-scenario-and-assumptions.md` states. Nothing in this note changes that baseline.

## 3. What the NPRM proposes

**How this was checked.** Each row was checked against the Federal Register text of the NPRM on 2026-10-03 or 2026-10-04, through the Federal Register's full-text search scoped to this one document, which returns verbatim passages. On 2026-10-04 the proposed regulatory text for the contingency planning, encryption, and backup and recovery rows was also read in full in the official printed version (90 FR 1015, 1017, and 1019). The last column says whether the passage was the proposed regulatory text or the preamble's description of it. The NPRM's amendatory instruction would "Revise and republish subpart C" in full and renumber much of it, so the citations below are proposed section numbers, not current CFR citations.

## 4. How the Contoso design lines up

This is a design-intent comparison against proposed text. It is not a compliance assessment, and the architecture set does not claim to meet the NPRM. Component IDs, residual risks (RR-), assumptions (A-), Conditional Access policies (CA-), work packages (WP-), and ADR numbers refer to the architecture set.

**Summary.** The design lines up best where the NPRM leans on Zero Trust staples: segmentation, MFA on administrator activation in the cloud, and real-time logging. It also requires encryption at rest for every data-store class, which matches the at-rest part of the proposed encryption standard except in how exceptions are justified. The largest gaps sit where the architecture set stops short on purpose: recovery has requirements but no restore-time target, frequencies, or implementation design (ADR-009), and patch timeframes and compliance audits are not designed. The sharpest tension is with medical devices: ADR-006 rejects active vulnerability scanning of medical devices for patient-safety reasons, while the proposal, as written, calls for automated vulnerability scans at least every six months.

Fit labels:

- **Aligned:** the design already provides the proposed control for the systems it covers.
- **Partly aligned:** the design provides part of it, or provides it with a different scope or cadence.
- **Gap:** the design does not address it.
- **Tension:** a deliberate design decision conflicts with the proposed text as written.
- **Out of scope:** the architecture set excludes the area by design.

**Notes to the alignment table.**

1. **Required versus addressable.**
   - Contoso design today: automatic logoff through the badge-removal lock and EHR inactivity timeouts (ADR-003; section 9 of `02-reference-architecture.md`), encryption in transit through per-app tunnels and TLS (ADR-002), and encryption at rest for every data-store class (section 7 of `02-reference-architecture.md`). Where a store cannot meet its at-rest requirement, the design records an owned, dated exception, documented as 45 CFR 164.306(d)(3) requires for an addressable specification. RR-15 does this for medical devices that cannot encrypt.
   - If finalized as proposed: Two cases stand out: the ZTMM roadmap's position that device segments compensate where medical device protocols lack encryption (`05-ztmm-maturity-roadmap.md`, Traffic Encryption), and RR-15's documentation of medical devices that cannot encrypt stored ePHI. Segmentation is not itself one of the proposed encryption exceptions, so both would depend on the exceptions described in the encryption row.
2. **Patch management, if finalized as proposed.** The exceptions cover a patch that is not available to address a risk identified in the risk analysis ((a)(4)(ii)(D)(1)) and an only available patch that would adversely affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of ePHI ((D)(2)). Where one applies, the entity would document it in real time and implement compensating controls ((E)), which is the role the design gives the tighter segment allow-list. The passages reviewed do not address manufacturer-controlled devices specifically.
3. **One-hour access termination, Contoso design today.** The revocation limits: continuous access evaluation acts on an account disable for CAE-capable Microsoft resources and, through Universal CAE, for zero trust network access (ZTNA) traffic from Windows clients; the EHR browser path is not CAE-enforced, so the EHR's own session lifetime governs (RR-03); and disabling an `IDS-AD` account stops new Kerberos tickets, while tickets already issued stay valid until they expire.
4. **Notice to other regulated entities within 24 hours, if finalized as proposed.** For example, `EXT-RCM-BA`, a business associate, would notify Contoso when one of its staff loses authorization to Contoso's EHR. That would turn the design's reliance on partner offboarding into a documented 24-hour obligation. Contoso would owe the same notice for any of its own workforce with access to another regulated entity's systems, which the design does not model.
5. **Restore critical systems within 72 hours.**
   - Contoso design today: ADR-008 defines continuity of access during cloud, internet, and component outages. Contoso owns its recovery time and point objectives and its restore order through its applications and data criticality analysis (164.308(a)(7)(ii)(E), the specification in force), and ADR-009 decision 3 requires that whatever objectives Contoso sets are checked against measured restore times. Implementation is not designed, so recovery capability stays unverified (A-19, RR-11), and Data Availability is deliberately not rated in the ZTMM map.
   - If finalized as proposed: The proposal would require written procedures to restore critical relevant electronic information systems and data within 72 hours of a loss, such as one caused by ransomware, and other systems and data in the order set by a criticality analysis ((ii)(A) and (D)). Meeting it would need the recovery design that ADR-009 defers and the criticality analysis left to Contoso, ranking `RES-EHR`, `RES-PHARM`, `RES-LIS`, `RES-PACS`, and `RES-INTEGRATION` together with the identity systems they depend on (`IDS-AD`, `PIP-PKI`), which ADR-009 keeps in scope. The proposal would also have the contingency plan reviewed and tested at least once every 12 months ((ii)(F)). ADR-008's failure-mode drills test continuity of access and, as ADR-009 notes, are not restore tests, so they would not by themselves show that the plan's recovery steps work.
6. **Business associate written verification, if finalized as proposed.** The proposal would require annual written verification from every business associate: `EXT-VENDOR-EHR`, each biomedical vendor with broker access (`EXT-VENDOR-BIOMED`, A-13), `EXT-RCM-BA`, and `EXT-TELEHEALTH`. For the partners whose home-tenant claims CA-14 and CA-15 accept (the EHR vendor, biomedical vendors with an Entra tenant, and the outsourcer), that verification would add documented assurance about the safeguards behind those claims.
7. **Encryption at rest and in transit.**
   - Contoso design today, at rest (section 7 of `02-reference-architecture.md`, data at rest by data-store class): provider encryption for all Microsoft 365 content, plus label encryption for labeled ePHI; volume encryption for `RES-FILES`, plus labels; full-disk or platform encryption on managed endpoints, checked by the compliance policy; app-layer encryption of organization data on personal phones under app protection (CA-09); guest-volume or database-layer encryption for the datacenter clinical stores (`RES-EHR` database tier, `RES-PACS` archive, `RES-LIS`, `RES-PHARM`, `RES-INTEGRATION`, `RES-LEGACY`); continuously verified platform encryption for `RES-AZ-WORKLOADS`; encryption, and deletion after confirmed receipt, for `RES-RCM-EXCHANGE`; encryption where the device supports it for `RES-IOMT`; and encrypted `SVC-BACKUP` copies whose keys can be recovered without production Tier 0 (ADR-009). Keys are held outside the systems they protect. `PEP-DATA` controls labeled content on endpoints but does not encrypt device storage. A store that cannot meet its requirement is an owned, dated exception (P-5), documented under 164.306(d)(3): medical devices that cannot encrypt (RR-15), legacy platforms on their ADR-005 retirement plans, and storage-layer-only encryption. WP-2.8 and WP-3.3 implement the requirements.
   - If finalized as proposed: The proposal would remove the addressable route that the design's exceptions use, so each would have to fit (b)(3). Medical devices that cannot encrypt (RR-15) would depend on the FDA device exceptions at (b)(3)(iv). For devices authorized through a submission received before March 29, 2023, that exception requires timely deployment of manufacturer updates and patches, which ties it to the patch management row. Legacy platforms would rely on the unsupported-asset exception at (b)(3)(i), which requires a written plan to migrate the ePHI to an asset that supports encryption; ADR-005's per-application retirement plans (owner, replacement path, target date) are the natural evidence. Each exception would need real-time documentation and compensating controls approved by the security official and reviewed at least once every 12 months or on environmental or operational change, whichever is more frequent ((b)(4)). RR-15 already names its compensating measures: segmentation, physical safeguards, location records in `PIP-ASSET-INV`, and media sanitization. The proposed text does not distinguish storage-layer from volume- or database-layer encryption, so the design's storage-layer-only exception reflects its own stricter requirement. Medical device protocols without encryption in transit would likewise need a documented exception, because segmentation is not itself one.
8. **Configuration management, if finalized as proposed.** Medical devices do not fit cleanly. Most cannot run anti-malware ((i); ADR-006), and many run operating systems that only the manufacturer may patch or reconfigure (A-11), so Contoso may be unable to configure them ((iii)) or disable their ports ((iv)) itself. Device-class segments limit which device ports can be reached over the network; the passages reviewed do not say whether that would satisfy (iv), and they show no device exception to any of the five specifications, so how manufacturer-controlled devices would be treated is unsettled.
9. **MFA, including privilege changes.**
   - Contoso design today: Every Tier 0 and Tier 1 administrator activation through PIM requires CA-06 reauthentication, and CA-04 and CA-05 confine administrator accounts to PAWs (section 4 of `03-identity-and-access.md`; ADR-004 decisions 4 and 6). Microsoft documents a 10-minute window: after a user reauthenticates for one activation, activating another eligible role within 10 minutes does not prompt again, across Microsoft Entra roles, Azure resource roles, and PIM for Groups (the PIM role settings page in the Sources table). Vendor engineers also elevate through PIM: they hold eligible membership in PIM for Groups session groups (`grp-vendor-ehr-session`, or a per-vendor `grp-vendor-biomed-<vendor>-session` group), and Contoso approves each activation (section 7 of `03-identity-and-access.md`; ADR-004 decision 8). The design states no activation-time MFA for those groups. Vendor MFA comes at sign-in instead: home-tenant phishing-resistant methods under CA-14 for B2B guests, or a Contoso-issued FIDO2 security key for Contoso-managed vendor accounts. The assumed starting point is MFA for every cloud sign-in, with phishable methods, while on-site Windows sign-in at shared clinical workstations is a password against Active Directory with no second factor (section 1 of `05-ztmm-maturity-roadmap.md`). Those workstations reach phishing-resistant MFA only when certificate badges arrive in phase 3 (WP-3.1). On-site access to `RES-LEGACY` relies on Active Directory authentication without Conditional Access (ADR-005).
   - If finalized as proposed: Two cases are partial. Vendor session-group activations also change privileges to relevant electronic information systems, since each grants broker access to `RES-EHR` servers or to that vendor's medical devices (F-06, F-07), but their MFA comes from the sign-in, not the activation. Tier 0 workload identities hold their application permissions as standing grants outside PIM, so the privilege changes they can make involve no MFA (RR-16; section 6 of `03-identity-and-access.md`), and the passages reviewed do not say how the proposal applies to non-human identities. The proposed text requires MFA "for any action that would change a user's privileges"; neither it nor the preamble's paragraph on (f)(2)(ii)(B) says whether MFA completed before the action, at sign-in or within Microsoft's 10-minute window, would satisfy that. Password-only on-site sign-in at shared clinical workstations would be the most visible gap until WP-3.1 (see the effective and compliance dates row). The proposed exceptions cover an asset that does not support MFA (given a written migration plan), emergencies, and FDA-authorized devices; the text reviewed does not say whether a phased badge rollout could rely on the first. The design does not state how MFA applies to privilege changes made directly in on-premises Active Directory. Legacy applications would need the written migration plans that the unsupported-asset exception requires, and medical devices would depend on the FDA device exceptions.
10. **Vulnerability scanning and penetration testing, Contoso design today.** `PIP-VULN` combines sensor-derived findings with manufacturer advisories; the advisories resemble the proposed monitoring of authoritative sources. Sensor-derived findings cover the three hospitals only: clinic devices have no passive sensor and are classified through NAC profiling and clinical engineering records (RR-14; ADR-006 decision 1). Defender for Endpoint discovery avoids active probing of medical device subnets (section 3 of `04-segmentation.md`). Penetration testing appears only for the segmentation controls around the cardholder data environment (PCI DSS v4.0.1 Requirement 11.4.5).
11. **Backup and recovery standards.**
    - Contoso design today: Recovery-plane administration must be isolated from Tier 0 administrative reach. Restores are tested on a schedule Contoso sets, from the immutable or offline copy, with evidence kept outside the recovery plane. Recovery-plane events, including failed or missed copy jobs, are monitored as Tier 0. The scope covers the clinical stores, `RES-LEGACY`, `RES-FILES`, `RES-AZ-WORKLOADS`, directory and domain controller system state (`IDS-AD`), certification authority material (`PIP-PKI`), and the keys that encrypted data depends on. ADR-009 sets no retention periods or test frequencies, and implementation is not designed (RR-11).
    - If finalized as proposed: The proposal would add fixed floors: copies no more than 48 hours old, monthly test restores of a representative sample, and backups of relevant electronic information systems whose controls are tested at least every six months. That last standard covers relevant electronic information systems, a proposed term that includes systems that otherwise affect ePHI, such as those that authenticate users (90 FR 927). ADR-009 covers directory and certification authority system state but leaves recovery of the cloud identity plane's configuration (`PE-IDENTITY` policies, `PIP-DEVICE-MGMT` baselines) as a candidate for the recovery design. ADR-009 goes further than the proposed backup text on immutable and offline copies and on separating recovery administration from production.

## 5. What remains unsettled

**Whether and when a final rule will issue.** The July 2027 date is an estimate that the agenda itself says is not binding, and Long-Term Actions placement means HHS does not expect action within 12 months of the agenda's publication. No final rule is under OIRA review. The NPRM has not been withdrawn, so a final rule, a substantially revised proposal, or a withdrawal all remain possible. This note does not predict which.

**What a final rule would say.** The NPRM asks for comment on many of its own specifics, including the proposed 72-hour restoration (whether 72 hours is right, whether it should cover all systems, whether some entities should restore faster) and the 12-month frequencies for inventory updates and contingency plan testing. Final text, frequencies, and section numbers could all differ from the proposal. Two internal citations in the NPRM also do not match its own regulatory text. The regulatory alternatives discussion places penetration testing at "164.308(h)(2)(iii)," while the proposed regulatory text puts it at 164.312(h)(2)(iii). The patch management discussion cross-references an exception at "(h)(2)(ii)(D)," which does not match the surrounding citations; the proposed regulatory text places the patch exceptions at 164.308(a)(4)(ii)(D), the citation this note uses.

**Cost and stakeholder positions.** HHS's own impact analysis estimated about $9 billion in first-year costs. In a letter dated December 8, 2025, more than 100 provider organizations and associations, including the American Medical Association, the Federation of American Hospitals, and the College of Healthcare Information Management Executives, asked the HHS Secretary to withdraw the NPRM "without further consideration." They cited "substantial new financial burdens" and "unreasonable implementation timelines." The letter states the signatories' position. It is advocacy, not a finding.

**Medical device questions that matter for Contoso.** These are open readings of the proposed text, not settled interpretations:

- **Scanning.** The passages reviewed show no medical device exception to the proposed automated vulnerability scanning, and they do not say whether passive, sensor-derived vulnerability identification would count as an automated scan.
- **Configuration management.** The passages reviewed show no device exception to any of the five proposed configuration management specifications, although most connected medical devices cannot run an anti-malware agent, and many can be reconfigured only by their manufacturer (A-11), which bears on the proposed configuration and network port specifications.
- **Patching.** The passages reviewed for the two proposed patch exceptions (no patch is available, or the only available one would adversely affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of ePHI) do not mention manufacturer-controlled devices. Whether a device that only its manufacturer may patch (A-11) fits either one is an open reading.
- **FDA device exceptions.** The proposed exceptions for encryption and MFA turn on the device's FDA submission date, the manufacturer's support status, and the entity's deployment of manufacturer updates. Using them would need per-device evidence from `PIP-ASSET-INV` and clinical engineering records. The design already records each device's encryption status in `PIP-ASSET-INV`, and each device that cannot encrypt as an owned, dated exception (RR-15), which would be the starting point.

**Secondary sources that overstate the status.** Some vendor and compliance-marketing summaries carry headlines that present these proposals as 2026 requirements or as a final rule, or that describe HHS as having corrected and republished the proposed text. The primary sources support none of those readings. "Revise and republish subpart C" is the NPRM's own proposed amendatory instruction, not a separate publication, and the Federal Register lists no other document under the RIN.
