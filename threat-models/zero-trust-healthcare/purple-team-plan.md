# Contoso Regional Health (fictional): purple team exercise plan

> **Reference work.** Fictional scenario built for a public portfolio. Not deployed in, derived from, or describing any employer environment. Not professional advice.

A purple team exercise plan for validating the detections and controls that answer the threat model. It defines objectives, scope and rules of engagement, a behavior list with the telemetry each behavior should produce and the control outcome it should trigger, success criteria, and a findings template. It is a validation design for a fictional environment, built from the attack paths in `attack-paths.md` and the backlog in `detection-priorities.md`.

This plan specifies behaviors and expected telemetry. It does not contain attack procedures, commands, payloads, or tooling configuration, and it is not an intrusion guide. Where a behavior needs to be executed to generate telemetry, the plan points to an existing, publicly maintained validation library by name. Selection and execution of specific tests is left to the operator, in an isolated lab the operator controls, in report-only or monitor-first mode, and never against live clinical systems or medical devices in patient use.

Technique references use MITRE ATT&CK Enterprise v19.2, verified against MITRE's official ATT&CK STIX data.

## 1. Objectives

1. Confirm that the Priority 1 and Priority 2 detections in `detection-priorities.md` fire on the behaviors they target, with usable fidelity and acceptable noise, including the emergency access alerts that `03-identity-and-access.md` section 5 requires (PX-17 and PX-20 for the exclusion group, PX-19 and PX-20 for the accounts).
2. Confirm that the preventive controls the architecture relies on (host east-west blocking, phishing-resistant MFA, token and session controls, segmentation, PIM activation) behave as designed, or surface where a phase gap leaves them inactive (TM-A2).
3. Measure the full detection-to-response loop, including whether the clinical safety gate and clinical notification steps (ADR-008) operate as designed.
4. Produce findings that feed back into the detection pack (`coverage-map.md`) and the architectural handback (`threat-model.md` section 9).

## 2. Scope

**In scope:** the detections and controls for attack paths AP-1 through AP-7, validated in an isolated lab or pre-production environment that mirrors the reference architecture's logical components. Identity-plane behaviors, endpoint behaviors, and network and segmentation behaviors are in scope for lab validation.

**Out of scope:**

- Any execution against production clinical systems, the production EHR, or live medical devices.
- Any execution against a medical device in patient use. Medical-device behaviors (AP-7) are validated on bench or lab equipment with clinical engineering present, consistent with the observe-first approach in `04-segmentation.md` section 3.
- Social-engineering tests against real staff beyond sanctioned, first-party phishing simulation (see the library note). Help-desk process validation (AP-2) is exercised as a tabletop and a sanctioned simulation, not by deceiving real staff without authorization.
- Supplier environments (`EXT-*`). Partner-path validation (AP-3) exercises Contoso's side of the trust boundary only.

## 3. Rules of engagement

- **Fictional and isolated.** The target is the reference environment, not any real organization. Execution happens in a lab the operator controls.
- **Monitor first.** Detections are observed in report-only or monitor mode before any response action is enabled, matching design principle P-6.
- **Clinical safety first.** No test may create a patient-safety risk. The clinical safety gate (ADR-008) is itself one of the things under test, and it is validated by tabletop and in the lab, never by disconnecting a device that could be in use.
- **Authorized windows and a named control contact.** Each exercise runs in an agreed window with a named person who can stop it.
- **Operator-run execution.** The operator selects specific tests from a public library and runs them personally in the lab. This plan does not provide the test content.

## 4. Validation libraries to draw from

For behaviors that must be executed to generate telemetry, use an existing, publicly maintained library and select the specific tests yourself. This plan names the libraries; it does not reproduce their content.

- **Atomic Red Team** (open source under the MIT license, published by Red Canary): a library of small tests mapped to ATT&CK techniques, suitable for validating endpoint and identity telemetry one technique at a time.
- **Apache Caldera (incubating)** (open-source adversary-emulation platform, formerly MITRE Caldera): for chaining behaviors into a path when a multi-step validation is wanted. MITRE announced its contribution to the Apache Incubator in May 2026, and the Apache Incubator records the project entering incubation on 2025-12-19.
- **Microsoft Defender for Office 365 Attack simulation training** (first-party; needs Microsoft 365 E5 or Defender for Office 365 Plan 2, and the scenario holds E5): for sanctioned phishing simulation against consenting test users or a pilot group, to exercise the delivery chokepoints of AP-1, AP-4, and AP-6 (`detection-priorities.md` item 14).
- **Microsoft Defender for Endpoint demonstration scenarios** (first-party): harmless demonstration files and steps that confirm attack surface reduction, next-generation protection, and endpoint detection and response detections, run on lab devices onboarded to Defender for Endpoint.

Map each selected test to the behavior row below by its ATT&CK technique ID. Keep the mapping in the findings record so coverage is traceable.

## 5. Behavior list with expected telemetry and control outcomes

Each row is a behavior to validate, the technique it represents, the telemetry a correct deployment should produce, the control outcome the architecture should show, and the success criterion. Rows are grouped by the attack path they validate. PX numbers are identifiers that other files cite, not a rank or a run order, so PX-16 to PX-20 sit in the AP-4 and AP-5 group, the paths they validate. Execute only in the lab, using a selected library test. Every detection rule named in this document is an untested template as of 2026-10-04 (`coverage-map.md` section 1).

**Notes to the AP-4 and AP-5 table.**

1. **PX-06.** Defender for Endpoint records the host's processes and logons (DeviceProcessEvents, DeviceLogonEvents) only if the lab host is onboarded to it, which the design does not yet state for `IDS-SYNC` (`threat-model.md` TM-A4). The Defender for Identity sensor on the host raises its own alerts (AlertInfo). AuditLogs record the hybrid change. Record the documented gap (a sync server outside Defender for Endpoint) as closed only if the lab host's Defender for Endpoint coverage reflects a design decision for `IDS-SYNC` (`threat-model.md` section 9 item 8); otherwise the run validates the Defender for Identity half only.
2. **PX-16.** AlertInfo and AlertEvidence carry the Defender for Identity alert from the sensor on the certification authority; the certification authority's audit log records the request and the issued certificate. DX-05 returning this single stage at Informational is the expected result, not a miss, because it escalates only when a second stage from the same entity falls within the same 24-hour span; to test that escalation, run PX-16 with PX-04 or PX-05 from the same lab account or workstation inside that window. If the request appears only in the certification authority's audit log, the missing rule on those events (`coverage-map.md` section 3, item 8) is recorded as a gap.
3. **PX-17.** Each change is made by an authorized administrator in a lab tenant: assignment of a Tier 0 directory role, a change to a report-only Conditional Access policy, a federation or cross-tenant trust change, and a test account added to the lab exclusion group (`grp-ca-emergency-access`) and then removed. The behavior cell lists one technique for each of these four changes, in the same order. More role assignments, each to a lab test account and removed afterward, mark the edges of SN-02's role lists: one of each role that `03-identity-and-access.md` section 4 says is never assigned (Partner Tier1 Support, Partner Tier2 Support, Windows 365 Administrator, and External Identity Provider Administrator; of these, Partner Tier2 Support can reset a Global Administrator's password), and one of a privileged role in neither of that section's lists: not a never-assigned role, and not one of its Tier 0 Entra roles, Domain Name Administrator included. Three more runs follow the tier lists: an assignment of Security Administrator, which `03-identity-and-access.md` section 4 places in Tier 0; an assignment of Application Administrator scoped to a single lab application; and a lab admin account removed from the lab `grp-admins-t0` and re-added, T1556.006 (Modify Authentication Process: Multi-Factor Authentication).

   AuditLogs record the actor's PIM activation, each role assignment naming the role, the policy update, the federation or cross-tenant access setting change, and both group membership changes, with the group identified by its object ID. The run also confirms which activity names a role-assignable group's membership changes log under, which the design has not yet checked, and records the activity name each role assignment logs under, because SN-02 matches role changes against a fixed list of activity names. The app-scoped assignment's activity name and target properties are recorded as the lab shows them, without assuming either in advance. The admin-group changes appear as rows whose TargetResources name the group's object ID.

   The Tier 0 activation is approved by one of two named approvers. The design requires an alert on the assignment of each never-assigned role, because it has no planned form of any of them (`03-identity-and-access.md` section 4), and SN-02's never-assigned branch raises it at High for each role on its list. The exclusion group is role-assignable with no owners, so its membership changes only through an active Privileged Role Administrator or Global Administrator assignment: an attempt by an administrator who holds a group-management role but neither of those (for example Groups Administrator, ADR-004 alternative F) is refused. Both group changes alert at Tier 0 priority although no policy changed. The admin-group changes need the same Tier 0 access as the exclusion group, and the design requires a Tier 0 priority alert on each (`03-identity-and-access.md` sections 3 and 4).

   The Tier 0 role assigned is one of the Tier 0 Entra roles in `03-identity-and-access.md` section 4, because SN-02 alerts on role changes only for its Tier 0 list and its never-assigned list; Privileged Authentication Administrator, which ADR-004 decision 1 places in Tier 0 and SN-02 lists among its Tier 0 roles, gets one run of its own to confirm the rule follows the design. The refused group change is recorded in the findings entry; Tier 0 activation cannot complete without approval. Each never-assigned assignment alerts at High with the actor and the account; one that the lab tenant accepts and that does not alert is recorded as SN-02's never-assigned list being behind 03 section 4. If the lab tenant does not offer a role or refuses its assignment, the findings entry records that, and if the tenant accepts none of them, the entry marks SN-02's never-assigned branch as unexercised. The out-of-set assignment is expected not to alert in SN-02 and is recorded as a coverage gap, not a miss. The Security Administrator assignment alerts like the other Tier 0 roles; if it does not, the findings entry records SN-02's role list as behind 03 section 4. The app-scoped assignment is recorded with its activity name, its target properties, and whether SN-02 alerted. Each admin-group change alerts at Tier 0 priority; one that does not is recorded against `detection-priorities.md` item 20. PX-17, PX-19, and PX-20 need no library test: authorized administrators make the changes.
4. **PX-19.** A sign-in with a lab emergency access account from the designated PAW, then a second announced sign-in from a lab device that is not a designated PAW, T1078.004 (Valid Accounts: Cloud Accounts). The drill's method change on a lab emergency account, T1098 (Account Manipulation), is PX-20's Temporary Access Pass issuance, which SN-07 alerts on, so PX-19 adds no step of its own for it: run it once and record the result under both IDs.

   SigninLogs record each interactive sign-in with the account's object ID as UserId; AADNonInteractiveUserSignInLogs may record further sign-ins as the session obtains tokens. PX-20 lists the AuditLogs rows for the method change.

   A sign-in from a device that is not a designated PAW is flagged as off-PAW. The method change raises a High SN-07 alert, the same priority as the sign-in alert (`03-identity-and-access.md` section 5; PX-20).

   PAW run: an alert for the account within the agreed time, with the session's alerts grouped into one incident and the off-PAW count (OffPawInteractiveSignIns in SN-06) at 0 on the alert that carries the interactive sign-in. Off-PAW run: an off-PAW count of 1 or more and the off-PAW severity text. Method change: PX-20's criterion for the pass issuance, recorded under both IDs. Every alert is closed against the drill record, not by a query exclusion.

   PX-19 is the validation drill that `03-identity-and-access.md` section 5 schedules. Microsoft's emergency access guidance describes such drills as confirming that monitoring and alerting rules trigger, with security-monitoring staff told beforehand, and as validating that the accounts can sign in and perform administrative tasks (Sources). With PX-20, it validates the emergency-access alerting named at `threat-model.md` TB-7: PX-19 the sign-ins (SN-06) for `attack-paths.md` AP-5 steps 8 and 8b, and PX-20 the changes to the group and to the accounts (SN-07) for steps 8a and 8b and TB-7's denial-of-service row. The off-PAW run exercises the branch of SN-06's severity logic that flags a sign-in from a device that is not a designated PAW. No Conditional Access policy enforces the PAW-only rule for these accounts, so that flag is its only technical check.
5. **PX-20.** Each change is announced to monitoring staff beforehand and made by an authorized administrator, against the lab exclusion group (set up like `grp-ca-emergency-access` in `03-identity-and-access.md` section 5: role-assignable, no owners, no role) and a lab emergency access account. Group, after an approved Privileged Role Administrator activation: a lab test account added as a member and removed, T1556.006 (Modify Authentication Process: Multi-Factor Authentication); then added as an owner and removed, T1098.003 (Account Manipulation: Additional Cloud Roles); and the group renamed. Account, after an approved Privileged Authentication Administrator activation: a Temporary Access Pass issued and deleted unused, T1098 (Account Manipulation); and the account disabled and re-enabled, T1531 (Account Access Removal). Inside the PX-19 drill, the signed-in lab emergency account makes one administrative change to a lab object other than the group and the two accounts, T1078.004 (Valid Accounts: Cloud Accounts). Two negative checks: an administrator who holds Authentication Administrator and no Tier 0 role tries the same pass issuance, and a lab Conditional Access policy that excludes the group is updated. Optional application step: an authorized administrator grants a lab application UserAuthMethod-TAP.ReadWrite.All as an application permission, the application issues a pass to the lab emergency account, T1098 (Account Manipulation), and the pass is deleted unused and the grant removed. The same step can carry the lab test that `03-identity-and-access.md` section 4 sets for the two read permissions Microsoft's reference also lists for the call: an application granted only UserAuthMethod-TAP.Read.All, then one granted only UserAuthenticationMethod.Read.All, each tries to issue a pass to a lab test user.

   AuditLogs record each PIM activation, which SN-02 also alerts on; every group change as a row whose TargetResources name the group's object ID; "Admin registered security info" with the status reason "Admin registered temporary access pass method for user" on the account, then rows for the pass's deletion, the disable, and the re-enable; and the emergency account's change as a row it initiated. The run records the activity names the lab shows for a role-assignable group's member and owner changes and for the pass deletion, and confirms that every account row carries the account's object ID, the only key SN-07 matches on (SN-06 also matches the user principal name). Optional application step: a row for the grant, and a row for the pass on the account whose initiator is the application, not a user; the run records the activity name and status reason the lab shows for an application-issued pass. For the read-permission test: a row for each grant, and a pass row or a refusal for each attempt.

   Group changes need an active Privileged Role Administrator or Global Administrator assignment, here an approved Tier 0 activation from a Tier 0 PAW. Account changes need at least Privileged Authentication Administrator, which Microsoft requires to change the credentials or sensitive attributes, such as the enabled state, of a role-assignable group's members, so the Authentication Administrator attempt is refused (ADR-004 Context; `03-identity-and-access.md` section 3). Every change raises a High SN-07 alert, the same priority as an emergency account sign-in, with no exclusion for planned work (`03-identity-and-access.md` section 5; ADR-004 decision 9). The policy update raises SN-02 and no SN-07 group alert. The optional application step needs no activation or approval (RR-16): SN-03 raises its Tier 0 grant alert, and SN-07's account alert, which labels the actor as an application with no PIM activation, is the only control on the issuance. Each read-permission attempt is expected to be refused; until the test shows that, a grant of either read permission counts as a Tier 0 grant (03 section 4).

   In the SN-07 alerts, group changes appear under the group, account changes under the account, and the emergency account's change as activity by an emergency access account, beside SN-06's alert for the drill sign-in. A change ingested in a later run can raise a further alert for the same object, which incident grouping joins when the entities match. Each alert names the actor and the activation behind the change (Privileged Role Administrator for the group, Privileged Authentication Administrator for the account); the pass issuance shows TapIssued at 1; each alert is closed against the change or drill record, not by a query exclusion. The refused attempt changes nothing; if it logs a failure row, SN-07 alerts on that row too, and the findings entry records which. The policy update returns no SN-07 group row and raises SN-02. Optional application step: SN-03 alerts on the grant at High, which also confirms that the permission name appears in the grant row (SN-03's validation notes), and SN-07 alerts on the issuance within the agreed time with RowsByApplication at 1 or more. TapIssued reads 1 only if an application-issued pass logs the same activity name and status reason as an administrator-issued one; record which. A refused issuance refutes the design's reading recorded in `threat-model.md` section 9 item 7: record it and route it to that section as an architectural finding against ADR-004's revisit trigger on application permissions. Read-permission test: two refusals settle 03 section 4's lab test, and an issued pass keeps that permission in Tier 0. Either way the result is routed to `threat-model.md` section 9, because ADR-004 lists this test as a revisit trigger.

   PX-20 shares runs rather than repeating them. Its member change is PX-17's exclusion-group step, its administrative change happens inside the PX-19 drill, and its Temporary Access Pass issuance can stand as PX-19's method change: run each once and record the result under both IDs. The owner step is there because Microsoft lets owners of a role-assignable group manage its membership, so an added owner could later change it with no Tier 0 activation, which makes an owner the durable form of step 8a. The policy check needs a lab policy that excludes the group: Microsoft's guidance says report-only policies need no exclusion, so PX-17's report-only change may not name the group. Confirm that the policy's audit row names the group's object ID; if it does not, the check shows nothing about SN-07's exclusion. SN-07 alerts on changes to the group and the accounts; PX-17, PX-19, and PX-20 exercise it.

## 6. Success criteria for the exercise overall

- Every Priority 1 detection (`detection-priorities.md` items 1 to 5) fires on its behavior within the agreed detection time, with a documented false-positive rate (item 5 for session replay only, because T1557 is not exercised), and the emergency access drill raises its alerts, with the off-PAW run flagged as off-PAW (PX-19), and every change to the lab exclusion group or a lab emergency account raises its SN-07 alert (PX-20).
- Every preventive control named above either blocks the behavior or produces a detection, and any that does neither is recorded as a gap with an owner.
- The clinical safety gate and clinical-notification steps operate as designed in the response tests (PX-02 and PX-13 to PX-15).
- Each finding is mapped to a detection rule file (for the detection pack's `coverage-map.md`) or to an architectural recommendation (`threat-model.md` section 9).
- No test caused a patient-safety risk, and the rules of engagement held.

## 8. Reporting and routing

- Confirmations of working detections update the detection pack's `coverage-map.md` status column.
- Missed or noisy detections become tuning work on the rule file that `coverage-map.md` names for the behavior.
- Control gaps that are architectural (not detection) are added to the handback list in `threat-model.md` section 9.
- Accepted residuals (for example RR-03 token theft, RR-04 device reach) are recorded against their residual-risk IDs rather than treated as new findings.
